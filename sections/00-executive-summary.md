# Executive Summary

Humanity is entering a polycrisis apocalypse. The word deserves its original sense: an unveiling, not an explosion. The evidence no longer supports the two stories that dominate public debate — sudden extinction by nuclear war or pandemic, and smooth adaptation to a warmer, busier planet. The more probable path is slower and worse than either story admits. A majority of the human population is being re-sorted into survivability risk regions: places that remain habitable in name but where pollution, climate stress, poverty, and conflict compound until life stabilizes at unsafe levels. Once trapped there, the population does not recover. It persists — for centuries, possibly millennia — in prolonged, grinding attrition, until accumulated failures terminate the species. That is the pessimistic branch this report documents, tests, and then refuses to treat as inevitable.

The inputs are already measurable. Humanity has breached six of nine planetary boundaries, including climate change and novel chemical entities ([Richardson et al., 2023](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adh2458)). Pollution kills roughly 9 million people a year ([Fuller et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2542-5196%2822%2900990-0)). Atmospheric carbon dioxide passed 425 parts per million and the planet runs above 1.2 degrees Celsius of warming ([NOAA, 2025](https://gml.noaa.gov/ccgg/trends/)). Roughly 700 million people live in extreme poverty and nearly half the world survives on less than $6.85 a day ([World Bank, 2024](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/poverty-prosperity-and-planet)). H Heuristics survivability modeling places 4.2 billion people inside the Survivability Risk Zone by 2050, with another 2.8 billion in the High-Stress Belt. These are not separate problems. They are one entangled system, and the entanglement is the apocalypse ([Lawrence et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/sus.2024.1)).

The demographic engine multiplies the exposure. Africa grows from 1.5 billion people in 2024 to roughly 3.8 billion by 2100, while Europe contracts from 745 million toward 590 million ([UN WPP, 2024](https://population.un.org/wpp/)). By the end of this century more than eight in ten people will live in Asia or Africa, and Africa will hold about 37 percent of the species. The median human being — the middle of the global distribution — shifts south with it. The consequence is not the crude claim that the average human becomes "less evolved." Evolution has no ladder, and populations do not sit on one. The measurable consequence is harsher: the median human will live inside the risk regions that already concentrate the world's air pollution, water stress, food insecurity, and state fragility. The civilization-wide problem is the mismatch between where humanity will live and where safe, wealthy, well-governed life is currently possible.

Haiti shows what that mismatch produces. A state with a proud founding history now suffers roughly 90 percent gang control of its capital, more than 5,900 killings in 2025, and 1.4 million internally displaced people ([UN News, 2025](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/07/1165246)). Haiti is a preview, not a prophecy: the mechanisms that collapsed it — institutional erosion, criminal governance, environmental degradation, and a trapped population — are the same mechanisms risk regions run on. Advanced weapons raise the stakes. Nuclear arsenals are modernizing rather than shrinking, with about 9,600 warheads in military stockpiles and roughly 3,900 deployed ([SIPRI, 2025](https://www.sipri.org/media/press-release/2025/nuclear-risks-grow-new-arms-race-looms-new-sipri-yearbook-out-now)). Autonomous and AI-enabled weapons lower the cost of organized violence precisely where institutions are weakest.

The chapters build the case in sequence. Chapter 1 defines the slow apocalypse and Chapter 2 maps the survivability risk regions where the majority will live. Chapters 3 through 5 establish the degradation arc, the demographic shift, and the middle-50 hypothesis. Chapters 6 through 8 test the mechanism against Haiti, advanced weapons, and societal turmoil. Chapters 9 and 10 compare the collapse trajectory with its counter-forces, and Chapter 11 closes with the binding term. A reader in a hurry should read this summary, the two scenario figures in Chapters 3 and 9, and the final chapter.

The report concludes with a specific claim, not a restatement. The polycrisis apocalypse is a demographic and environmental re-sorting that traps the majority of the species inside survivability risk regions, stabilizes it at unsafe levels, and only then decays toward extinction. The binding term follows. Judge every long-run policy by one test: does it move the median human out of the risk region, or merely manage their suffering inside it? Suffering management buys time. Moving the median human buys survival.
